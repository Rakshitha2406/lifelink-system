Connecting Blood Needs to Suitable Sources, Faster.

LifeLink is a centralized emergency decision-support platform designed to eliminate the chaotic, time-consuming manual search for compatible blood during life-threatening medical emergencies. By uniting compatibility rules, verified stock, urgency tiers, and location-based scoring, we bridge the vital minutes between critical trauma calls and transfusion.

Community Service Project — Problem & Solution Matrix

Community Problem Traditional Manual Approach LifeLink Smart Solution
1. Difficulty in Finding Compatible Blood Attendants make panic phone calls to friends, relatives, and unorganized blood centers. Automated Component-Aware Matching: Clinically evaluates RBC, Platelet, and Plasma rules instantly to find safe matches.
2. Scattered Availability Information Blood stock is trapped in disparate hospital registries and paper logbooks with zero visibility. Centralized Directory & Real-Time Tracking: Participating facilities share standardized, searchable inventory levels.

Interactive Project Test Scenarios

The project prompt specifies six critical real-world edge scenarios to demonstrate during evaluation. Click any card to automatically configure and execute the algorithm for that test case.

TEST CASE 1 Optimal Match
Nearby Source Has Sufficient Stock

Input: Hospital at Gudlavalleru requires 2 units O+ PRBC.
Expected Result: Gudlavalleru Community Blood Bank (0.5 km) is ranked #1 with high compatibility and score > 90.

TEST CASE 2 Shortage Allocation
Nearest Source Has Insufficient Units

Input: Hospital needs 8 units B- PRBC for a vascular emergency.
Expected Result: Nearest center has only 2 units; multi-source pooling activates to pull from secondary sources.

TEST CASE 3 Mass Casualty
Several Sources Needed to Cover Request

Input: 12 units O- required for multi-vehicle collision trauma code red.
Expected Result: Greedy knapsack pooling aggregates scarce universal donor units across multiple regional centers.

TEST CASE 4 Verification Check
Source Has Not Confirmed Its Stock

Input: Query for group A- with nearby facility that is unverified.
Expected Result: Unverified center is flagged with warning badge and down-scored; telephone confirmation prompt displayed.

TEST CASE 5 Zero Inventory
No Suitable Source Is Available

Input: 15 units rare AB- Cryoprecipitate requested.
Expected Result: Total regional stockout detected; system refuses to fabricate false matches and displays Apex State Escalation protocol.

TEST CASE 6 Critical Urgency Surge
Critical Request Receives High Priority

Input: Pediatric shock trauma (Critical) vs Scheduled surgery (Normal).
Expected Result: Algorithm heavily weights proximity and rapid dispatch zone, triggering high-priority alert dispatch.

Abstract

This project aims to develop a smart and centralized platform that helps patients and hospitals quickly find suitable blood sources during emergency situations. The system considers important factors such as blood group, required blood units, hospital location, urgency level, and blood availability to identify nearby compatible sources. It uses location-based and priority-based matching to connect emergency blood requirements with suitable blood banks, hospitals, or registered sources. The platform also helps reduce the time and effort involved in manual blood searching and communication. Overall, the proposed system aims to provide a faster, organized, and reliable approach to emergency blood matching, thereby supporting better emergency response and community healthcare services.

Problem Statement

Context: During medical emergencies, finding the required blood group within a short time can be difficult. Hospitals and patients often depend on manual searches, phone calls, and personal contacts.

1. Difficulty in Finding Blood Finding rare or matched blood groups quickly during emergencies is severely challenging.
2. Scattered Information Availability data is fragmented across isolated blood banks without unified digital visibility.
3. Time Delay (Golden Hour) Manual calling wastes precious minutes while trauma patients require immediate transfusion.

Project Objectives

  • Quick Blood Matching: Automatically identify clinically compatible sources based on component rules.
  • Location-Based Search: Compute Haversine distances to recommend nearby centers.
  • Urgency-Based Prioritization: Dynamically weigh distance and stock during Critical Code Red scenarios.
  • Multi-Source Shortage Pooling: Split large blood requisitions across multiple facilities when one center has insufficient units.
  • Reduce Search Time: Slash emergency search delays from hours to seconds.

Project Scope

  • Blood Requirement Registration: Collect blood group, component, units, location, urgency.
  • Directory & Availability: Real-time inventory tracking across blood banks & hospital storage units.
  • Decision-Support Algorithm: Multi-factor scoring (Distance 35%, Stock 35%, Affinity 15%, Verification 15%).
  • Workflow Lifecycle: Submitted → Contacted → Confirmed → Fulfilled.
  • College Service: Demonstrating social technology for rural and semi-urban healthcare clusters.

📱 Next Steps: Mobile Application Upgrade Path

The next iteration of this CSP project will package the LifeLink client as a cross-platform mobile app using Flutter or React Native. This will enable push notifications directly to on-call transfusion technicians and ambulances, GPS tracking for blood delivery couriers, and one-tap emergency SOS broadcasts for disaster response teams.
